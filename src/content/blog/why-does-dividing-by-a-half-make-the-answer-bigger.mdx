Division often seems to make an answer smaller. So why does 2 ÷ ½ give 4?

Here is the question we’ll learn to solve: **how many half-metre pieces can we cut from a 2-metre ribbon?** We’ll count the pieces first, then connect that count to 2 ÷ ½.

## Count the half-metre pieces

A <ReadingTerm term="Half" definition="One of two equal parts of a whole. Half a metre is 0.5 metres, or 50 centimetres.">half</ReadingTerm> means one of two equal parts. Cut the first metre of ribbon into two equal pieces. Each piece is half a metre long. Do the same with the second metre, and you get two more pieces.

That makes **four pieces altogether**.

So the answer to our question is **four half-metre pieces**. We write the calculation as **2 ÷ ½ = 4**.

Here, <ReadingTerm term="Division" definition="When the size of each group is known, division tells us how many such groups fit into the total. Here, the groups are half-metre pieces.">division</ReadingTerm> tells us how many pieces we can make. The 2 is the ribbon’s length in metres. The ½ is the length of each piece in metres. The answer, 4, is the number of pieces.

## Smaller pieces, more pieces

The ribbon has not grown. We get a bigger count because we are making smaller pieces. Every 1-metre piece can be cut into two half-metre pieces, so the number of pieces doubles.

<KeyIdea id="halves-key-idea">Dividing by ½ counts halves. Each whole contains two halves, so the count doubles.</KeyIdea>

This explains the shortcut “divide by ½, multiply by 2”. Each whole contains two halves, so we multiply the number of wholes by two to count the halves.

## “Divide by two” asks a different question

Now take the same 2-metre ribbon and ask for **two equal pieces**. Each will be 1 metre long. This is 2 ÷ 2 = 1.

The questions are different. In 2 ÷ 2, we choose the number of equal pieces and find their length. In 2 ÷ ½, we choose the length of each piece and find how many we can make.

For our original question, each piece must be half a metre long. Four such pieces fit into 2 metres, so **2 ÷ ½ = 4**.
