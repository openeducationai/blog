We’ll learn how to solve this question:

**Let a = i + j + k and b = j − k. A vector c satisfies a × c = b and a · c = 3. Find c · (a − 2b).**

Adapted from [JEE Main, 6 April 2026 Shift 1 Q23](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s3f8e59f4b2fe7c5705bf878bbd494ccdf/uploads/2026/04/202604092007095665.pdf#page=8), question ID 6952782158. Here i, j and k are unit vectors along the three coordinate axes. The letters a, b and c denote vectors. You need vector addition and simple algebra; we will explain both products.

## A dot product measures alignment

Imagine two arrows u and v drawn from the same point on a flat sheet. Their lengths are |u| and |v|. If the angle between them is θ, their dot product is u · v = |u||v| cos θ. The result is a number, not another arrow.

If the arrows are perpendicular, θ = 90° and cos θ = 0. Their dot product is therefore zero. For nonzero vectors, a zero dot product also tells us that they are perpendicular. The zero vector has zero dot product with every vector, although it has no direction.

In coordinates, the same dot product is uₓvₓ + uᵧvᵧ + u_zv_z. For instance, (1, 0, 0) · (0, 1, 0) = 0. The two coordinate-axis arrows meet at a right angle, so the geometry and calculation agree.

## A cross product points out of the plane

The cross product u × v is a vector. When u and v are not parallel, their [cross product is perpendicular to both](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-3/pages/2-4-the-cross-product). In our sheet picture, it points straight out of the sheet or straight into it. The order of the two input vectors chooses the direction by the right-hand rule.

Call this cross product n. Since n is perpendicular to each input, n · u = 0 and n · v = 0. This is the useful connection: a cross product creates a perpendicular vector, and a dot product with either input then vanishes.

<ArticleDiagram kind="crossNormal">The blue arrows u and v lie in the shaded plane. The orange arrow n = u × v is normal, or perpendicular, to that plane. Thus n · u and n · v are both zero. This is a schematic perspective drawing.</ArticleDiagram>

If the two input vectors are parallel, their cross product is the zero vector. The two dot products are still zero. We should not assign a direction to that zero result.

<KeyIdea id="cross-dot-zero">A cross product has zero dot product with either of the vectors that formed it: (u × v) · u = 0 and (u × v) · v = 0.</KeyIdea>

The magnitude gives another piece of information: |u × v| = |u||v| sin θ. For unit vectors, both lengths are one, so this reduces to sin θ. If an angle is stated to be acute, a known sine determines its positive cosine. Without the acute-angle condition, the cosine could have either sign.

## Expand only what the question asks for

Dot products distribute over addition and subtraction. For example, c · (a − 2b) = c · a − 2(c · b). This follows directly by multiplying corresponding components. Also, c · a = a · c: swapping the inputs leaves a dot product unchanged.

The opening question already gives a · c. It also describes b as a cross product. Before solving three unknown components of c, check whether those two facts determine the dot products in the expanded expression.

Keep the two multiplication symbols distinct. A dot product returns a number and can be swapped. A cross product returns a vector and changes sign when its inputs are swapped: u × v = −v × u.

## Remove a perpendicular term before solving

Suppose a vector A is built as A = λu + v + (u × v), where λ is an unknown number. Taking its dot product with u removes the cross-product term. We get A · u = λ(u · u) + v · u.

Taking its dot product with v removes that term again: A · v = λ(u · v) + v · v. If u and v are unit vectors, u · u = v · v = 1. We have turned the vector expression into two ordinary equations.

Notice that u · v need not be zero. Unit vectors have length one; they are not necessarily perpendicular. The supplied angle information decides their dot product in the first exercise.

## Try the past questions

**1. Unit vectors u and v make an acute angle and satisfy |u × v| = √3/2. If A = λu + v + (u × v), express λ in terms of A · u and A · v.**

<details class="reading-check"><summary>Show solution</summary><p>Since the vectors are unit vectors, sin θ = √3/2. The angle is acute, so cos θ = 1/2 and u · v = 1/2. Substitution into the two expressions cancels the constant terms, leaving λ.</p></details>
