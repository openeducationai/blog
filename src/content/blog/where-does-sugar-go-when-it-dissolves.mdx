Stir a small spoonful of sugar into a glass of water. After a while, you cannot see the grains. Yet the water tastes sweet. Where did the sugar go?

Let’s follow one grain of sugar. We’ll see what changes when it dissolves, and how the sugar can become visible again.

## Look inside a grain

A sugar grain contains an enormous number of tiny <ReadingTerm term="Molecule" definition="A group of atoms joined together. Sugar and water are made of molecules, far too small to see with your eyes.">molecules</ReadingTerm>. These molecules are held together in a crystal. The grain is big enough to see; a single molecule is not.

Water is made of molecules too. When water touches the grain, water molecules attract sugar molecules at its surface. Sugar molecules separate from the crystal and mix among the water molecules.

**Each sugar molecule stays whole.** It is the collection of molecules in the grain that is coming apart.

Follow a pink dot in the picture. It starts among the others, then spreads out into the water. The same dots are there at the end.

## Spread out, still sugar

As more molecules leave, the grain gets smaller. Eventually, there is no visible grain left. The sugar molecules are now mixed through the water, too small for your eyes to pick out.

This mixing is called **dissolving**. The sugar and water form a <ReadingTerm term="Solution" definition="A mixture in which the dissolved substance is spread evenly. In this glass, sugar molecules are mixed throughout the water.">solution</ReadingTerm>.

The water tastes sweet because it still contains sugar. Being clear does not mean a liquid contains only water.

<KeyIdea id="sugar-key-idea">When sugar dissolves, its molecules spread through the water. The sugar is still there.</KeyIdea>

## Can we get the sugar back?

Imagine the water slowly evaporating from the glass. Water leaves as vapour, while the sugar stays behind. As enough water leaves, sugar molecules can gather into crystals again.

The new crystals need not look like the original grains. But they are made of the sugar that was in the glass all along.
