Stir a small spoonful of sugar into a glass of water. After a while, you cannot see the grains. Yet the water tastes sweet. Where did the sugar go?

We’ll follow one grain to answer that question. Then we’ll see how we could get the sugar back.

## Look inside a grain

A sugar grain is made of many tiny <ReadingTerm term="Molecule" definition="A group of atoms joined together. Sugar and water are made of molecules, far too small to see with your eyes.">molecules</ReadingTerm>. They are packed together to form the grain. You can see the grain because it contains so many of them. Each molecule on its own is too small to see.

Water is made of molecules too. When they touch the outside of the sugar grain, they pull on the sugar molecules there. Some sugar molecules leave the grain and mix with the water. As this continues, the grain gets smaller.

**The sugar molecules stay whole.** They separate from one another, but they do not turn into water or disappear.

## Spread out, still sugar

Eventually, the grain is gone from sight. Its sugar molecules are spread through the water. You cannot see them because they are so small.

We call this **dissolving**. The mixture of sugar and water is called a <ReadingTerm term="Solution" definition="A mixture in which the dissolved substance is spread evenly. In this glass, sugar molecules are mixed throughout the water.">solution</ReadingTerm>.

The water still tastes sweet because the sugar is still there. A liquid can look clear even when something is dissolved in it.

<KeyIdea id="sugar-key-idea">When sugar dissolves, its molecules spread through the water. The sugar is still there.</KeyIdea>

## Can we get the sugar back?

Imagine leaving the glass while the water slowly dries up. The water goes into the air as water vapour. This is called evaporation. The sugar stays in the glass.

As more water leaves, the sugar molecules can gather together again and form crystals. These may look different from the grains we started with, but they contain the same sugar.
